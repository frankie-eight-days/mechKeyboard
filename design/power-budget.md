# Power and charging budget

## Current schematic values

The captured high-current setting proposed for firmware is 1.04 A at 4.18 V with an 80 mA termination setting. Qualified USB lighting uses 150 mA and battery lighting 100 mA. The earlier 1.28 A and 200 mA scenarios below are retained as sizing comparisons. The current circuit decisions and limits are in [charge control](charge-control.md), [USB input limiting](usb-limit.md), and [RGB power](rgb-power.md).

## Display and volume control

The display branch is supplied from the nominal 3.3 V rail through U1000. See the [connector and panel headroom calculation](display-and-volume.md#voltage-at-the-connector-and-glass) for the downstream LDO dropout and unresolved charge-pump limit. Adafruit gives about 20 mA average and approximately 15 mA in practice depending on the lit pixels. Reserve **35 mA at 3.3 V** for preliminary active-mode budgeting, including display bus pull-ups. The allowance is a design assumption. At 85% logic-rail conversion efficiency this adds up to 136 mW at SYS. With 90% charger-path efficiency and 4.75 V input that means about **32 mA extra at USB**. The existing 1.25 A charge-plus-lighting example becomes approximately **1.28 A**, close to the 3 A advertisement tier’s conservative 1.30 A operating allocation. See the U800 thresholds for the lower tiers. U1000 also charges the module capacitors. For 20 µF effective input and a typical 2.7 mV/µs ramp, budget a roughly 54 mA transient on top of running current when enabling it. This is a typical-condition estimate. Prioritize HID, reduce RGB or charging for the display, and keep the display off until adequate current has been granted. The original USB transient model already includes abrupt removal of the full 1.30 A normal budget. A continuously lit display at the 35 mA assumption draws an additional 42 mA from a 3.2 V cell at 85% conversion efficiency. Blank the display on inactivity. [Display and volume](display-and-volume.md) · [OLED current characteristics](https://www.adafruit.com/product/4440) · [USB source policy](usb-limit.md)

The 10 kΩ potentiometer at 3.3 V draws 330 µA nominal or 413 µA at minimum resistance while excited. Power it from POT_EXC during sampling. At a 2 ms pulse, 20 times per second, the divider contribution is at most 17 µA averaged over time, excluding ADC/MCU operation. Force the display off and the pot excitation low during no-cell startup, suspend and deep sleep. Include the new load switch’s off-state leakage in the unresolved whole-board suspend budget. [Alps potentiometer](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/e/products/detail/RK09K1130A6S/) · [TI load switch](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps22919.pdf)

## Optional WASD feedback

The rev F haptics sheet adds a SYS-powered DRV2605L and one 47 µF local tantalum, plus ceramic bypass. Budget 0.22 W at SYS during each hit. On USB this translates to roughly 51 mA at 4.75 V or 55 mA at 4.45 V, at 90% efficiency. Motor power and the shared I²C path use separate rails, with a shutdown-capable translator on the bus. The previous 1.5 A connector-only current estimates are comparative. Use the actual U800 tier limits below for accessory decisions. [Haptics calculation](haptics.md) · [USB current limiter](usb-limit.md)

|---|---:|---|
| Unqualified or suspended USB | Cold-start / suspend budget | Haptics held off |
| Configured 500 mA host | 0.18 A | OLED and haptics, with RGB and charging off. About 0.129 A total at J200. |

The estimates include a 10 mA bypass reserve, 85% RGB/OLED conversion efficiency, a modeled +5.5% charge-current error and full OLED demand. Because BQ25622 VINDPM acts at the chip input, it can fold charging back well before the connector reaches 4.45 V. In USB suspend and low-cell conditions keep haptic EN low. For battery operation allow 69 mA at 3.2 V SYS during a hit, or 3.4 mA averaged over one 50 ms hit per second. Keep the existing nominal 1.04 A charge profile for haptics-off fast charging on a qualified source. A full charge near two hours is still a design target. The modeled 0.80 and 0.88 A profiles consume about 1.88 h and 1.70 h of ideal constant-current time for 1.50 Ah before taper. Local SYS sag, release overshoot, asleep leakage and upstream response are part of the haptics review and require final-layout verification. [Firmware and power details](haptics.md)

## Initial sizing study

Architecture basis for the 67-key keyboard. The charge-time target is about two hours from ordinary low-battery shutdown to charge termination on a qualified USB-C wall supply, at room temperature. All efficiencies, allocations, brightness caps and durations below are design assumptions. Cell approval, component tolerances and enclosure thermals remain open.

The first KiCad capture uses the onsemi FUSB303BTMX and the BQ25622 charger. Elevated current requires a separate hardware input limiter. [USB-C schematic](../electronics/usb-input.kicad_sch) · [onsemi datasheet](https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/fusb303b-d.pdf)

## USB and cell charge

Treat the USB selector as a HID routing control. Both modes may enumerate on a data host, allowing Bluetooth typing and a configured USB power budget simultaneously. Authorization is keyed to each physical attach and bus state. USB 2.0 default power, an advertised Type-C current, and an explicit USB PD contract have distinct rules. This revision uses positive Type-C current detection and keeps VBUS at 5 V. [USB-IF power overview](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/D1T1-2%20-%20USB%20Type-C%20System%20Overview.pdf) · [USB-IF interoperability procedure](https://usb.org/sites/default/files/3.2%20Interoperability%20Testing%20v1.03%20w%20USB%20Type-C.pdf)

Use 4.75 V at the receptacle for this comparison, 90% input-to-SYS or battery efficiency, 85% SYS-to-LED conversion, and 0.12 W at SYS for active logic and housekeeping. At the 200 mA LED ceiling, system power is approximately 1.30 W (5 × 0.20 / 0.85 + 0.12). Current usable for charging near 4.20 V equals approximately (4.75 × allowed input current × 0.90 − system power) / 4.20. Round charge setpoints **down** to the available hardware steps. Characterize the selected circuit at low VBUS, short-circuit, hot ambient and current-limit tolerance corners before choosing final settings.

## Lighting and battery current

The OPSCO SK6812MINI-E-012 data give 12 mA typical, 14.5 mA maximum **per color** under their test conditions, 0.6 mA typical powered/black at 4.5 V, and 3.7–5.5 V operating voltage. At 5 V the specified high threshold is 3.25 V. Reserve 1 mA per powered pixel as a provisional static allocation. The sheet specifies typical static current only. It also limits three-color operation to 70% grayscale. [OPSCO datasheet, pp. 3, 6](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/datasheet/pdf/0907a34a701b4aef568078003f6e9eae.pdf?productCode=C5149201)

| State | 5 V LED branch | Whole-board implication |
|---|---|---|
| Full black, rail still on | 67 × 0.6 ≈ 40 mA typical | Always cut the rail during sleep and for a persistent lights-off setting. |
| All channels at code 255, fault estimate only | 67 × (0.001 + 3 × 0.0145) ≈ 2.98 A | Forbidden as normal animation. Design short-circuit/fault protection independent of frame data. |
| USB active, qualified source | 200 mA average rail budget | At 67 mA baseline, 133 mA remains for channels. Uniform white would permit only ~4.6% duty per color, about code 11/255. |
| BLE, battery-powered | 100 mA average rail budget | At 67 mA baseline, 33 mA remains. Uniform white would permit ~1.1% duty per color, about code 2/255. Limit models should apply to the sum of all channel codes, allowing brighter colors on a subset of keys. Perceived legend brightness remains unknown. |
| Battery near low cutoff | 0 mA commanded | Turn lights off before the pack protector trips, with hysteresis to prevent on/off cycling. |

Per frame, budget mA as 67 × 1 + 14.5 × Σ(channel code / 255), summing every RGB channel. Apply it after all color correction and animations, across firmware startup and diagnostics. The model limits **average** current. If pixel PWM aligns, short peaks can approach the fault estimate even for modest frame averages. Evaluate pulsed load, slew rate, return paths and effective capacitance against the converter and limiter response. For example, 2.7 A beyond the converter response for 10 µs at 0.20 V allowed droop would require ~135 µF effective local storage. Pulse duration and alignment remain open, so this example does not size the capacitor bank. A fault should shut down lighting while preserving the keyboard logic rail. Consider staggered regional enables or a lower-current emitter if adequate optical output and power margins cannot coexist.

For a 3.7 V cell, with 100 mA RGB at 5 V and 0.12 W SYS housekeeping, modeled cell current is (0.50 / 0.85 + 0.12) / 3.7 ≈ 191 mA. At 3.2 V it rises to ~221 mA. At nominal voltage a continuous 1500 mAh / 191 mA gives ~7.8 hours before derating. With RGB off, 0.12 W is an **active upper allocation**, about 32 mA at 3.7 V. A 2 mA average BLE-typing design goal would give 750 h of ideal capacity; it requires a separate scan/radio duty-cycle budget. No runtime values are measured. For travel sleep, aim at ≤50 µA from the cell including the charger, CC detector, regulators, matrix, dividers, GPIO pulls and RGB off leakage.

## Interfaces and schematic closure

- Controller owns PMIC_SCL, PMIC_SDA, CC_OUT1, CC_OUT2, CHG_INT_N, CHG_REQUEST, USB_SUSPEND, HOST_500_OK, RGB_EN, RGB_PG and RGB_DATA. The charger uses 3.3 V I²C. Leave the FUSB303B ADDR/ORIENT pin open for GPIO mode and use its 900 kΩ PORT-to-ground sink strap. Charger ADC can supply the cell-voltage reading. Reserve an independent BAT_ADC if the final low-battery strategy needs it. The charger owns cell NTC sampling.
- Pull CE inactive and RGB_EN low during reset. In FUSB303B OUT1/OUT2 order, H/L means detached, H/H means default attach, L/H means 1.5 A and L/L means 3 A. Its sink Rp-change debounce is 10–20 ms. The input limiter must revoke elevated current on detach or downgrade even with stalled firmware. Gate any USB 500 mA grant on actual host configuration and clear it on bus reset or suspend. Bound direct VBUS loads and inrush at default attach. The charger's ILIM range starts at 100 mA and its published scale-factor tolerance is characterized at 1.6 A. Include this tolerance when specifying the external connector limit. [FUSB303B tables 1, 7 and 8](https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/fusb303b-d.pdf) · [BQ25622 electrical characteristics](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq25622.pdf)
- Prove startup with USB only, a depleted pack, and a disconnected pack. On suspend with a valid cell, BQ25622 high-impedance mode disconnects its input. With an absent/depleted cell this would remove SYS power. For that case keep a VBUS retention path and budget, for example, 1.2 mA charger/logic retention + 0.7 mA USB PHY/sensing + 0.3 mA CC and leakage = 2.2 mA target, leaving 0.3 mA margin. Replace these allocations with component limits. If unsatisfied, add a dedicated retention path before schematic release.
- The detector and its pull-ups use the 3.3 V logic rail. Account for the static current with one output low in detached travel mode. Verify USB-only startup with unpowered logic and the detector's dead-battery Rd, plus the intermediate codes as the rail rises or falls.
- Confirm the selected cell's fast-charge curve and NTC beta/connector polarity for the actual orderable harness. Establish a supplier-supported cell current and termination at the nominated temperature before enabling the fast setting. Use independent hardware bounds for cell voltage, temperature and short-circuit protection. Firmware grants stay capped by the physical source and pack.
